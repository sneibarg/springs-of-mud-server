# ROM24 parity review of player titles

This review follows titles set by players and staff through storage and display in the room, `who`, `whois`, and `score`. It includes the title identity selected when a player switches into a mobile. The earlier profile review covered the self-title tilde difference. This review extends the trace to staff edits, rendered output, and switched sessions. Level-up title tables, general roster filters, and other `string` fields are outside scope. [Earlier profile review](archive/repository-rom24-parity-2026-11-03-aftermath-profile-review.md)

## Confirmed runtime defect

### A switched player can prevent the roster commands from rendering

ROM24 checks visibility of the controlled body, resolves the descriptor's original player, then checks that player's visibility and renders their name and title. Springs' `switch` stores the original player in session metadata and puts the mobile into `session.character`. The common roster helper reads that mobile and accesses `cloaked`, a field on player characters. A public-interpreter probe with Bob in a switched session raised `AttributeError` in `who`, `whois Bob`, and `whois Alice`. Even looking up an unrelated player fails because candidate collection runs before name filtering. For an observer who can see both Bob and the mobile, the ROM24 roster still renders Bob and his title. ## Verified display differences

### Who, whois, and score insert a space that the stored title already accounts for

Both self-title setters store an ordinary title with one leading space and omit that space when the title begins with `.`, `,`, `!`, or `?`. ROM24 concatenates the name and stored title directly. Springs adds a space of its own in the shared roster formatter and the score formatter. Public-command probes rendered `Alice  the explorer` after `title the explorer` and `Alice , the explorer` after `title , the explorer`. ROM24 renders `Alice the explorer` and `Alice, the explorer`. All four punctuation prefixes follow the same branch. Springs-generated level titles use a bare `the …` string, so removing the extra separator without handling that stored form would affect generated titles. ### Standing player room lines omit the title

With Alice standing, Bob standing off furniture and BRIEF disabled, and Alice's title set to `the explorer`, ROM24 appends Alice's title before the posture suffix. It skips the title when Bob has BRIEF enabled, Bob is on furniture, Alice is in a different posture, or an applicable long description has already been emitted. Springs renders `Alice is here.` in both tested standing cases with BRIEF on and off. A resting Alice also has no title in either implementation. The probe used the room's real player-list renderer. Whether Springs deliberately omits titles from this view remains unconfirmed. ## Self-title and staff override

The full self command agrees on a missing-argument prompt, silent handling while switched into an NPC, success feedback, and a 45-character cap for ordinary ASCII text. The bundled staff `string` command runs at the same relative trust requirement as ROM24 (`L5`). Both accept the abbreviated `ti` field, refuse an NPC title target in the title branch, and allow a staff title longer than 45 characters. The 60-character staff override was retained through a Python character serialization round trip. ROM24 writes the title as a tilde-terminated field and reapplies the leading-space convention after reading it. Two input differences were reproduced and their design intent remains unresolved.

- With 44 ordinary characters followed by `~Z`, ROM24 self-title truncates before converting `~` to `-`, so the final character is a hyphen. Springs drops the tilde before truncating, so it retains the `Z`. The staff route adds a third behavior. `string char Bob title a~b` stores `a-b` in ROM24 and preserves `a~b` in Springs. [ROM24 self-title rewrite](C:/Users/scott/PycharmProjects/PyRom/Rom24/src/act_info.c:2402) · [ROM24 staff rewrite](C:/Users/scott/PycharmProjects/PyRom/Rom24/src/act_wiz.c:3709) · [Springs self setter](C:/Users/scott/PycharmProjects/springs-of-mud-server/src/interp/commands/Info.py:517) · [Springs staff setter](C:/Users/scott/PycharmProjects/springs-of-mud-server/src/interp/commands/Wiz.py:825)
- ROM24 accepts `string cha Bob title …` because `cha` is a prefix of `character`. Springs recognizes `char`, `character`, and `mobile` as exact type names and returns syntax for `cha`. This is a type selector within the command. ## Validation

Ruff ran read-only against five Python files. Rule counts and concise locations narrowed the source reads to title handling, roster collection, and rendering. The lint findings did not establish the parity differences. The probes used the bundled command records, the production interpreter, and in-memory sessions and connections. All 49 tests passed across four focused suites. ROM24 expectations came from the local C source. Neither server was exercised over a live socket, and the title round trip did not write to the external character service.
